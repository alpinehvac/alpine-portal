What it is

A fixed-fee, on-site assessment of a building’s HVAC and BAS that produces a scored risk report and a 12–24 month prioritized plan with budget ranges, delivered in a debrief meeting that ends with a maintenance proposal.

Who it’s for

Property and facility managers of commercial, industrial and institutional buildings with complex HVAC and BAS who are under-served, in the dark, or over-paying with their current contractor.

Zero-risk mechanics

  1. Fee credit: 100% of the audit fee is credited against the first year of an Alpine maintenance agreement signed within 45 days of the debrief.
  2. Findings guarantee: if the report doesn’t contain at least 3 actionable findings the client agrees are worth acting on, the fee is refunded in full, deposit included.
  3. Ownership: the client keeps the report whether or not they sign. It is useful to them with any contractor.

Deliverables

  1. Pre-call (20 min) — qualify the building and decision-maker
  2. On-site assessment (2–4 hrs) — HVAC, BAS, energy, comfort, vendor/compliance
  3. Building Performance Scorecard (1 page, scored out of 100)
  4. CLEAR written report — risk register, photos, 12–24 month plan, budget ranges
  5. Debrief meeting (45 min) — walk-through plus maintenance proposal

1Qualification RulesNatalie · Jake▸
Book an audit only when 4 of the 6 criteria are met.

Qualified

  1. Commercial, industrial or institutional building (not residential, not a small strip unit with 1–2 RTUs)
  2. At least 4 major pieces of HVAC equipment, or a central plant
  3. Has a BAS/controls system (any brand, any condition), or is planning one
  4. Contact is the decision-maker or can bring the decision-maker to the debrief
  5. Current contractor relationship has a pain (cost, visibility, response, trust), or no maintenance contract at all
  6. Inside the service area — within 150 km of downtown Hamilton

Service area rule

  1. Core (Niagara–Hamilton): any qualified building.
  2. Outer ring (GTA and beyond, up to 150 km): Complex or Portfolio buildings only. The account must justify the travel.
  3. Over 150 km: do not book.

Disqualifiers — do not book

  1. Wants a free quote for a single repair or replacement, no interest in ongoing care
  2. Locked into a contract with 12+ months remaining and no pain
  3. New construction or large projects (not Alpine’s model)
  4. Decision-maker refuses to attend the debrief and won’t name who decides

5Pre-Call (20 Minutes)Jake▸
Qualify the building and decision-maker, introduce price and zero-risk terms, take the deposit, book the visit and debrief.

1. Frame — 1 min

“Thanks for making time. Here’s what I’d like to do: ask a few questions about the building, tell you how the audit works and what it costs, and if it’s a fit we’ll book the visit. If it’s not a fit, I’ll tell you. And just so you know up front — if we don’t find at least three things worth acting on, you don’t pay. Fair?”

2. Building — 5 min

  1. Building type, size (sq ft), age, number of floors / buildings
  2. Major equipment: RTUs, boilers, chillers, cooling towers, AHUs, VAVs, heat pumps, specialty (data/server rooms, pools, process)
  3. BAS: brand/platform (Metasys, Niagara, Delta, etc.), who services it, is anyone actually using it
  4. Tenants/occupancy: anything critical (data, healthcare, food service, labs)

3. Pain — Uncovering Value — 6 min

  1. “Why look at this now?”
  2. “What’s the last HVAC problem that caught you off guard? What did it cost — money, tenant complaints, your time?”
  3. “How do you know today what your contractor actually did on their last visit?”
  4. “If a major unit went down tomorrow, who’s affected and what does that cost?”
  5. “What’s your HVAC budget process — do you know next year’s numbers or guess?”
Label it“So it sounds like [pain 1] and [pain 2] — and the real problem is you don’t have visibility until something breaks. Is that right?”

4. Decision — 2 min

  1. “Who else is involved in deciding on HVAC service?”
  2. “When does your current agreement renew?” (if any)
  3. “If the audit shows a clear plan, what would stop you from acting on it?”

7.2 Equipment checks

Rooftop units / packaged

  • Age vs expected service life (~15–20 yrs)
  • Cabinet, panels, gaskets, curb/seal condition
  • Filters — type, condition, last change date
  • Coils — evaporator and condenser cleanliness, fin damage
  • Belts/sheaves, bearings, motor condition
  • Economizer operation, damper linkage, actuator
  • Refrigerant circuit — signs of leaks/oil; pressures if running
  • Heat section — burner, heat exchanger visual, flue, combustion test where safe
  • Condensate drain, pan, trap
  • Electrical — contactors, wiring, disconnect
  • Controls — local stat vs BAS-controlled

Boilers / heating plant

  • Age, condition, nameplate
  • Combustion analysis (where accessible and safe)
  • Safety controls, relief valves, low-water cutoff
  • Venting / flue condition
  • Pumps, expansion tank, water treatment
  • TSSA certificate / inspection status current and displayed

Chillers / cooling towers

  • Age, condition, refrigerant type (flag phase-out refrigerants)
  • Operating log present; approach temps if available
  • Tower fill, basin, drift eliminators, water treatment program
  • Leak check records

Air handlers / VAV / terminal units

  • AHU filters, coils, fans, dampers, drain pans
  • Sample VAV boxes (min. 10% or 5 boxes) — damper, reheat valve, sensor vs actual
  • Duct / insulation condition where visible

Specialty

  • Server/data room cooling — redundancy, alarming, temp/humidity
  • Pool / dehumidification units
  • Make-up air, kitchen exhaust interlocks
  • Heat pumps / split systems

7.3 BAS / controls review

  • Platform and version — supported or end-of-life
  • Who has access; logins documented
  • Graphics accurate and usable
  • Schedules match actual occupancy; any units running 24/7
  • Manual overrides / points in hand — count
  • Active and suppressed alarms — count, age, anyone responding
  • Setpoints — deadbands, simultaneous heating and cooling
  • Sensor sample — 3–5 BAS readings vs handheld
  • Trend logs enabled; any history
  • Which equipment is on the BAS vs standalone
  • Remote access / alarm notifications to anyone

7.4 Energy

  • 12-month utility history reviewed (if provided) — kWh/sq ft and m³ gas/sq ft vs building type
  • Equipment running outside occupied hours
  • Simultaneous heating and cooling
  • Economizers disabled / stuck
  • Lighting / HVAC schedule mismatch
  • Building ≥50,000 sq ft → ask whether they file Ontario Energy & Water Reporting (O. Reg. 506/18)

7.5 Comfort / IAQ

  • Tenant complaint areas — spot-check temp/humidity
  • Hot/cold spots, drafts, noise
  • Ventilation — outdoor air dampers open and operating
  • Optional: loggers in 2–3 problem zones, collect at debrief

7.6 Vendor & compliance

  • Current maintenance agreement — scope and frequency
  • Evidence of maintenance — service tags, filter dates, logs, reports
  • Equipment clearly not serviced per stated scope (photograph it)
  • TSSA certificates current (boilers, pressure vessels, fuel-fired appliances)
  • Refrigerant leak-check records / logbooks where required
  • Backflow preventer test tags current (if in scope)
  • As-builts, sequences of operation, BAS points list available

Agreement scope build

  1. Audit asset register becomes the agreement equipment schedule — no re-survey
  2. Hours calculated in the Service Agreement Calculator from the asset register
  3. “Now” items quoted as separate repair proposals at agreement rates
  4. “Next” and “Plan” items logged in the customer portal as future recommendations with budget ranges

Signing → onboarding checklist

  • Agreement signed; audit credit schedule noted on billing (even split across first-year invoices)
  • Credit recorded in QuickBooks / BuildOps
  • Customer portal live within 3 business days; audit report + scorecard uploaded
  • Asset register loaded into BuildOps as equipment list
  • First maintenance visit within 15 business days
  • “Now” repair proposals sent within 3 business days of signing
  • Service manager introduced; quarterly review dates set
  • First visit: meet or exceed — fix at least one visible finding from the audit
  • 30-day check-in call by Jake: “How’s it going vs what we promised?”
Reinforce the decision: every quarterly report re-scores the building against the audit baseline. Show the score improving.
