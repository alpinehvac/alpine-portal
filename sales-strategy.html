Closer Framework

Hormozi's CLOSER framework, adapted to how we sell service agreements — use it to run a discovery-to-close conversation in order, without skipping steps.

C
Clarify

What is important to you? What are your goals? What is the problem? What is your dream outcome?

L
Label

"So it sounds like you are (insert current state), and you want to be (insert dream state), and X is the thing that's in the way. Does that sound right?"

O
Overview the Pain

"So I am assuming I'm not the first person you've come to, to solve this problem. What have you done in the past? Why did that not work for you? What did you like? What did you dislike?"

Associate with what they liked, and disassociate with what they disliked. Go back to the top and ask "what else, what else, what else" — this is the pain cycle.

S
Sell the Vacation

The 3-Pillar Pitch — each pillar is 1–2 statements followed by an analogy or metaphor.

1. Peace — Organization
Show the customer portal: past jobs, upcoming live schedule, storage of quotes, jobs, and maintenance records.
2. Stability — Predictability
More frequent, consistently scheduled site visits mean more overall touch-points — increasing the likelihood of prevention and decreasing unexpected breakdowns or damage.
3. Rapport — Education
Regular calls with their account manager to explain past and future jobs, giving the facility manager an understanding beyond the job report or estimate — so the operator can make informed decisions for their facility.
E
Explain Away Concerns

Common objections to get to the bottom of, answer, and close the sale:

"I don't have time" "I don't have money" "Someone else has to make the decision" "This isn't right for me" "I didn't like a similar product" "I am not ready"
R
Reinforce the Decision

This is the handoff between the sale and when they begin. All you need to do is make it clear what the next steps will look like, then keep your promises — make sure those exact things happen when you said they would. This reinforces your ability to keep promises, and confirms right away that our company is on top of things — building their "Perceived Likelihood of Achievement."

  1. 1Start date: the first site visit needs to meet or exceed expectations.
  2. 2Customer portal set up with schedule within 3 business days.
  3. 3Confirm billing info / contact.

Why It's Structured This Way

Our business model is built around incentivizing customers to commit to long-term service agreements. Predictable, scheduled visits let Alpine operate more efficiently — and that efficiency is what allows us to pass lower effective rates back to agreement customers instead of pricing every visit at a reactive, one-off rate.

The Building Assessment + Consultation and First-Response Flat Rate are both funnel offers, not standalone products. Each is meant to point down the funnel toward a signed service agreement:

Assessment / First-Response → Service Agreement Offered → Signed & Prioritized

If a customer declines the service agreement after a First-Response visit, non-contract rates apply to any future calls, and those calls are not placed in priority sequence. This keeps the incentive structure intact — the agreement is the only path to both lower rates and priority scheduling.
