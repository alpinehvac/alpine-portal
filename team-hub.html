Performance Pay

Profit Sharing

At Alpine, when the business does well, the people who built that result share in it.

How It Works

Technicians at 3rd year and above are eligible to participate in Alpine's profit sharing program. At the end of each period, a portion of net profit is distributed to eligible team members. This is not a discretionary bonus — it's a structured share of the outcome tied directly to how the business performed.

The intent is simple: the people doing the work should benefit when the work pays off. Profit sharing is reviewed annually and details are discussed individually during onboarding.

      Accountability & Daily Expectations
      Call recording & analysis: Every sales call is recorded and reviewed. This is non-negotiable — it's how we develop the role and protect the company. Any discomfort with this is disqualifying.
      Weekly team meetings (×2 minimum): Attendance is mandatory, not optional. The BDR is part of the team, not independent of it.
      Daily huddles at 7:15am: When required, attendance is expected consistently — not when convenient.
      Hours set by the market, not by you: This role does not have a 9–5. Availability is dictated by when clients and prospects are reachable.
      Client gifts & packages: Purchased at the BDR's expense as a relationship investment — not reimbursed by Alpine.
      Self-Development Requirements
      Books: 1 per month minimum — sales, mindset, business. This is expected, not suggested.
      Courses: 1 or more per year minimum — relevant to the role, self-directed.
      Continuous improvement mindset: Alpine invests in people who invest in themselves. Stagnation is a culture mismatch.
      Compensation Reality — Year 1 & Beyond
      Year 1 base ($40K) is temporary: The base salary exists to prove the model and establish the strategy — not as a permanent structure. Once the concept is proven, the role transitions to full commission. Candidates must be genuinely motivated by this, not just tolerating it.
      Commission split at payout: 50% at contract signing, 50% at 6 months. No lump sum on close — cash flow patience is required.
      Self-generated vs. company leads: 30% on self-generated leads, 5% on company-provided leads. The gap exists because the heavy lifting is different — owning your pipeline is the expectation, not the exception.
      Company Inbound Leads — Policy
      Company-generated inbound leads are provided at leadership's discretion — they are not automatic, guaranteed, or part of the base expectation of this role. The BDR is a hunter first. Inbound support from Alpine is a bonus when it occurs, not a baseline to rely on.
      Candidate must demonstrate comfort building their own pipeline from zero — inbound leads are supplemental only.
      5% commission on company leads reflects the reduced effort in sourcing — candidate must understand and accept this distinction.
      Territory & Contact Ownership
      All contacts belong to Alpine: Every relationship, lead, and client developed during employment is company property. No book of business leaves with the BDR. This is non-negotiable.
      Territory: Formal boundaries are assigned when a second BDR is hired. Until then, the BDR operates across the full territory (150km radius centred on Hamilton). No exclusivity guarantee exists at this stage.
      CRM discipline: All contacts must be logged in BuildOps immediately. The pipeline belongs to Alpine, not to any individual's memory or personal spreadsheet.
      Closing Question
      After walking through all of the above: "Having heard all of that — the accountability, the transition to full commission, the contact ownership policy, and the self-development expectations — are you more or less excited about this role than when you walked in? And why?"

      A great answer gets more specific and more energized. A poor answer hedges, deflects, or focuses on the negatives just surfaced. Trust the response.

        Foreman Standards

        What the Foreman designation means at Alpine — in plain language, with real examples. Use this as a living reference for foreman meetings and ongoing performance conversations.

        The Foreman add-on (+$5.00/hr) is a designated role with real accountability, not automatic tenure. The four standards below define what it looks like to earn and keep that designation.

        01 Bring solutions, not problems ▼

        A Foreman removes burdens from leadership, rather than adding to them. He handles his crew and tasks effectively, with leadership's best interest in mind.

        ✓Looks like
        1

        "Hey, the ductwork we ordered turned out to be wrong. I sourced the fittings we needed from another supplier — our original one couldn't get it done in time, so I made the call to keep the project moving rather than save a few bucks. In the meantime we got most of the square duct hung, hangers and rod in the ceiling prepped for those fittings, and got started on the next phase. Tomorrow I'd like to have my apprentice continue here while I head to another site to keep the ball rolling on our weekly objective. Any changes you'd like to make to that plan?"

        2

        "We showed up on site and don't have access to the scissor lift we needed. I got my apprentice going on demoing equipment we could access without the lift, while I sourced another one from a rental company in town. They dropped it off in good time and we were still able to complete the majority of the work planned for today. Most of the two-man work is done now, so we can divide and conquer tomorrow. I've ordered all the parts needed to finish the repairs — the apprentice will pick those up in the morning while I head to another site to keep the planned work on schedule. Any changes you'd like to make to that plan?"

        3

        "I know we had a plan for this particular unit, but I noticed a couple of items that might justify tagging it — venting, gas fittings. I looked up the code while I set the apprentice up to continue work. Some of those infractions are legitimate. The venting needs to be replaced, and the gas valve as well. I've ordered the materials and noted them for repair on our next visit. Any changes you'd like to make to that plan?"

        ✗Doesn't look like
        1

        "The ductwork that we ordered is wrong. I've got 3 fittings here that won't work. What do you want me to do?"

        2

        "We showed up on site and don't have access to the scissor lift we need to complete the job. What do you want me to do?"

        3

        "I know we had a plan for this particular unit, but do you really want it repaired? What do you want me to do?"

        02 Responsible for crew productivity ▼

        A Foreman is responsible for the maximum efficiency and productivity of himself and his crew. Every day.

        ✓Looks like

        At the weekly meeting, expectations are set for reasonable weekly progress on jobs, projects, and maintenance events. The leader and Foreman both agree on a plan, sorting through as many details as possible during that meeting. Throughout the week, pre-established daily check-ins are in place — morning and evening — where updates are shared and strategy changes discussed if necessary.

        At the end of the week, if goals are not met, a clear and tangible reason is identified. This is discussed at the upcoming weekly meeting with the goal of it never happening again. As we learn and grow, we cannot tolerate making the same mistakes twice. The purpose of planning the week in detail is to see potential hurdles and address them well before they become reality. This system, properly implemented, makes us more efficient every single day.

        ✗Doesn't look like

        At the weekly meeting, leadership lays out the plans for the week. Few questions are asked about potential problems, and technicians head to work. Immediately, the Foreman is greeted on site with hurdles that could have been avoided — scheduling conflicts, missing materials, equipment not ready.

        The Foreman spends the rest of the day sourcing material and managing these hurdles while the apprentice watches, doubling the lost productivity. They call the leader for help, even though leadership has other obligations. At the end of the week, goals are not met, no clear reason is identified, and nothing is fixed for next week — resulting in the same outcome the following week.

        03 Master delegation and personal organization ▼

        A Foreman needs to manage both his physical work productivity and his administrative duty. It is not one or the other — both need adequate attention in harmony. Creating peace among the team and customers, not chaos.

        ✓Looks like — CLEAN
        • Clean truck
        • Clean job site
        • Safe job site
        • Clean inbox
        • Clean notes and records
        • Clear communication with customer before and after every visit
        • Clear communication with team members and leaders
        ✗Doesn't look like — UNCLEAN
        • Unclean truck
        • Unclean job site
        • Unsafe job site
        • Unclean inbox
        • Unclean or non-existent notes and records
        • Unclear or non-existent communication with customer
        • Unclear or non-existent communication with team members and leaders
        04 Communication ▼

        A Foreman is an excellent communicator, and knows the appropriate times to communicate — facilitating maximum efficiency across the crew, leadership, and the entire team.

        ✓Looks like

        Communication is limited to Morning Huddles and daily checkouts. With the exception of emergencies and major schedule changes, the plan for the week has already been established. Any hurdles or changes that require a strategy shift are handled by the Foreman, then communicated to the leader at the designated check-in points — morning and evening. Mid-day communication is limited as much as possible, allowing for maximum efficiency across the board.

        ✗Doesn't look like

        Foremen are constantly on the phone with team members or leadership to overcome hurdles that could have been predicted and prevented with proper planning. This breaks the productivity of the Foreman, his crew, and leadership simultaneously — a compounding drain on the entire team's output.
